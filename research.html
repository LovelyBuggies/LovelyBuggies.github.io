Optimizing LLM Collaboration with MARL

Frontier language models can act as agents that reason and interact with their environments to solve complex tasks. These LLM agents can play different roles and collaborate to improve overall performance and efficiency. We study both centralized and decentralized collaboration, with agents either relying on centralized information or making decisions independently. We design and analyze multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) algorithms, including MAGRPO, CoLLM-CC, CoLLM-DC, MADPO, and MARLHF, to optimize LLM collaboration across tasks such as game playing, basic programming, simple tool use, embodied navigation, and web planning [1,2,3,4]. We find that, with appropriate fine-tuning, small language models can learn effective collaboration strategies and work together to outperform a larger model.

Mean-Field Games for Autonomous Vehicle Navigation

Mean-field game (MFG) theory studies decision-making in multi-agent systems with very large populations. It connects stochastic modeling and distributed control. We study MFGs for autonomous vehicle navigation, where each vehicle agent makes decisions about velocity control and route choice based on the current population density [1]. The actions of all vehicles jointly drive the evolution of population density, and this process repeats until it converges to an equilibrium. We have proposed various approaches to address practical challenges related to fine granularity [2], scalability, and computational efficiency [3,4].
